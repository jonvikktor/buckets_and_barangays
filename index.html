Buckets & Barangays

Mapping basketball courts across the Philippines.

Jon Viktor D. Cabuenas

In the Philippines, three Bs stand out: boxing, beauty pageants, and basketball.
Out of the three, basketball has become deeply ingrained in everyday life. From makeshift backboards nailed to trees to covered courts at the heart of barangays — the country's smallest government units — hoops can be found almost everywhere.

Ask a first-time visitor to count the basketball hoops they pass on a drive through the Philippines, and they'll likely lose track before the journey ends.

"Of all the places that I've traveled, this has so much passion and enthusiasm for the game."

— Kobe Bryant

Artist: @themayacarandang || Instagram

Philippine hoops appear in various forms — a rim nailed to a tree, a painted court on public pavement, or a covered court at the center of a barangay. Parking lots become makeshift courts, while quiet residential streets transform into playing surfaces when traffic dies down. Sometimes even if it doesn't. Many of these informal spaces never make it onto a map.

"Anywhere you look you can see groups of kids playing on a makeshift basket on a telephone pole, on the side of a building, in 3 feet of rainwater... It's a powerful visual."

— Erik Spoelstra, Filipino-American NBA coach

But what does the craze look like on a map?

In the Philippines, basketball spills into neighborhoods, schoolyards, parks, and village plazas. From improvised hoops to full-sized covered courts, the game is woven into daily life.

A nation built around the hoop

At least 24,000 basketball courts have been mapped across the Philippines, from outdoor courts and covered gyms to neighborhood hoops documented by volunteers. Countless more informal courts may exist but have yet to be recorded.

Metro Manila

The capital region, home to 17 cities and municipalities, has 2,192 mapped courts, outdoor pitches and covered gyms alike.

Who has the most courts?

Larger cities tend to have more basketball courts because they have more people. Metro Manila turns out to be uneven, and on the whole, has less courts than the national rate — 21.3 / 100k — once population is accounted for.

15.7 / 100k

Metro Manila's rate, versus 21.3 nationwide.

How dense are they?

Measuring courts per square kilometer highlights where basketball infrastructure is physically concentrated. Mandaluyong ranks near the bottom in courts per capita but climbs to second in court density because of its compact size.

5.71 / km²

Mandaluyong's density, vs. 13.95 per 100k residents.

Taguig

Among Metro Manila's cities is Taguig, home to Bonifacio Global City and one of the country's most recognizable basketball courts. Hidden inside the Tenement housing complex in Western Bicutan, the court has become an icon in Philippine basketball culture.

The Tenement

Tucked between rows of apartment buildings, the Tenement basketball court has become a symbol of Philippine basketball culture. The public housing complex has welcomed global athletes and filmmakers, with its court being repainted several times — including murals honoring Kobe and Gianna.

Seen from above

The Tenement court looks no different from thousands of others scattered across the Philippines. That's the point.

A nationwide network

While only a handful of courts have gained international recognition, countless others continue to quietly serve the same purpose — a venue for communities to come together one game at a time. Together, they form a nationwide network that reflects the country's passion for the game.

This dataset captures only courts that have been mapped, and thousands of improvised hoops and informal spaces remain invisible. A reminder that the country's basketball story extends beyond what any map can show.

Methodology

Court counts and locations come from live queries to OpenStreetMap's Overpass API, cross-checked against each city's actual administrative boundary rather than a bounding box. Population figures are from the Philippine Statistics Authority's 2024 Census of Population. Full methodology and code: basketball_courts.ipynb.

Done as a project for the Lede Program at Columbia University.

Data: OpenStreetMap Overpass API (basketball courts), Philippine Statistics Authority 2024 Census of Population (population, land area).
